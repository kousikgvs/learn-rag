Amazon is a multinational technology company best known for e-commerce, Amazon Web Services (AWS), digital advertising, logistics, devices, and streaming services. The company was founded by Jeff Bezos in 1994, and Andy Jassy served as chief executive officer in 2023. Amazon's operating model combines first-party retail, a large third-party marketplace, subscription services such as Prime, and a fast-growing cloud business through AWS.

In 2023, Amazon reported approximately $574.7 billion in revenue and about $30.4 billion in net income. The year reflected a strong recovery in profitability after the weaker 2022 period. Amazon continued to focus on operational efficiency, cost discipline, and improving fulfillment speed across its retail network.

AWS remained one of Amazon's most strategically important businesses in 2023, supporting enterprise cloud workloads, data infrastructure, machine learning, and generative AI adoption. Amazon also continued expanding its advertising business and strengthening Prime's role in customer retention. Across the broader company, management emphasized customer obsession, long-term investment, and continued innovation in fulfillment, cloud services, and AI-enabled experiences.

Additional operating context for Amazon in 2023:

Amazon's business model in 2023 can be viewed as a mix of several large engines: online stores, third-party seller services, AWS, advertising, subscription services, and physical stores. This matters because Amazon is not only a retailer. It is also a platform company that earns fees from marketplace sellers, monetizes demand through sponsored product advertising, and uses Prime to increase purchase frequency and customer loyalty.

The marketplace remained a major strategic asset. Third-party sellers use Amazon's storefront, payments infrastructure, and logistics network to reach customers. Fulfillment by Amazon, often abbreviated FBA, lets merchants store inventory in Amazon facilities while Amazon handles picking, packing, shipping, customer service, and some returns workflows. Amazon's logistics footprint continued to be a core differentiator in 2023. The company operated a large network of fulfillment centers, sortation centers, delivery stations, and transportation assets across multiple regions. Its logistics stack included Amazon Air, Amazon Freight, Amazon Logistics, Flex drivers, and same-day or next-day delivery capabilities in selected markets. Management's efficiency focus in 2023 was partly about getting more output from that network while lowering cost to serve.

The consumer ecosystem around Amazon extended beyond the main shopping site. Prime combined shipping benefits with digital media services such as Prime Video and Amazon Music. The company also owned businesses and brands including Whole Foods Market, Audible, Twitch, Ring, IMDb, and MGM-related media assets. Devices and services such as Kindle, Echo, Alexa, Fire TV, and Fire tablets helped Amazon stay present in households outside the checkout flow.

AWS in 2023 was central to Amazon's enterprise narrative. It provided infrastructure and platform services for storage, compute, databases, analytics, security, application development, and AI workloads. Historically important AWS services include Amazon S3 for storage and Amazon EC2 for compute, and by 2023 AWS was also a major platform for machine learning and generative AI experimentation. The AWS business is strategically important because it tends to carry much higher margins than first-party retail.

Amazon also continued investing in automation and robotics across operations. Warehouse automation, inventory routing, software orchestration, and robotics-assisted handling were all part of the company's broader effort to improve speed and productivity.
